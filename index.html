    The Six-Day Box

    Sunday is not our business

    Six days of breakfast, lunch, an evening bowl and dinner. Arranged in order, so the only decision left is which sleeve to open.

    We stop at six on purpose. Sunday is when the house cooks together, and no box should be in the middle of that.

    Every meal arrives as two packets. The first is the food. The second is a small sachet of cold-pressed gingelly or ghee, infused with asafoetida, that you pour in after the lid comes off — because that is when you can still smell it.

    Two Friends

    Karur, Tamil Nadu

    Most of these recipes were never written down. They were watched, and then they were forgotten.

    We started this the way most people start cooking again — by realising we had stopped. Between work and everything else, the food we grew up on had quietly become weekend food, then festival food, then nothing.

    So we began collecting. Asking mothers and grandmothers and neighbours what they used to make on an ordinary Tuesday. Most of it had no recipe. It had a method, a smell, and a rough handful.

    Then came the harder part: turning a rough handful into something that fits in a packet and still tastes like the thing it came from. That work is what we are doing now, in Karur, one recipe at a time.

    We are not making these easier than they were. We are making them possible on a Tuesday again.

    Questions

    The ones we get asked

    Is anything wet in the packet?

    No. Everything is dry — flours, grains, dried vegetables and ground spice. The only liquid is the small oil or ghee sachet that goes in at the end, and that is sealed separately.

    What do I still need at home?

    Water, and salt if you like more of it. A few recipes ask for curd, which we say on the packet. Nothing else. We would rather tell you plainly than promise a shorter list than we can keep.

    I live in a hostel with only a kettle.

    Then eighteen recipes are open to you today — every rasam, soup, kanji and porridge we make. Boiling water is the only requirement.

    How long does it keep?

    We are still finishing shelf-life testing, and we will print a real date rather than an optimistic one. Anything with coconut in it keeps for less time than the rest, and we will say so on those packs.

    Are there preservatives?

    No. The food is dry, which is how it was always kept. Roasting, drying and a good seal do the work.

    Can I choose my own six days?

    Not at first. We are launching with a fixed rotation of six weeks so we can learn what people actually finish. Choosing your own is what we build next.

    Do you ship outside India?

    Not yet. Dry food travels well and we intend to, but we would rather get it right in Tamil Nadu first.

    We are still cooking. Come in early.
